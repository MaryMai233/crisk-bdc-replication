\noindent\textbf{Abstract.}
This paper replicates CRISK for ten large U.S. banks and asks why its portfolio validation is less precise for business development companies (BDCs). Mean bank climate beta rises by 0.230 in 2020 (standard error 0.029), while replicated top-four $m$CRISK and CRISK increases recover 85.3 and 86.5 percent of published magnitudes. The original Y-14 validation uses millions of loan-level observations and remains significant at 1 percent across committed, utilized, unlevered, and firm-matched specifications. Public BDC filings instead provide 380 aggregated firm-quarters. An estimator-aligned DCC--FF49 portfolio beta yields a firm- and quarter-fixed-effects equity coefficient of 0.152 (standard error 0.085), conventionally significant at 10 percent but not under wild-cluster inference. Expanding the post-KOL continuation from five holdings to a pre-specified 15-security basket covering 77.1 percent of official N-PORT holdings raises daily KOL tracking from 0.454 to 0.790, yet the coefficient is 0.184 (standard error 0.114). Thus the BDC precision gap is not explained by basket breadth alone; it primarily reflects disclosure resolution and limited portfolio variation. The maintained climate scenario reduces the mean statutory asset-coverage buffer by 5.85 percentage points without a breach.

\section{Introduction}

CRISK maps a traded stranded-asset factor into dynamic climate beta and bank capital shortfall \citep{JungEngleBerner2025}. I replicate its bank benchmark and examine two implementation questions: how to measure the portfolio channel in publicly traded private credit, and how to maintain the factor after KOL liquidates. The application is to business development companies (BDCs), whose binding capital rule is statutory asset coverage rather than a bank capital ratio.

Three results follow. First, mean bank climate beta rises by 0.230 in 2020 (SE 0.029); all ten banks move in the predicted direction, and top-four $m$CRISK and CRISK increases recover 85.3 and 86.5 percent of the published magnitudes. The daily maximum follows the November vaccine announcement, so the annual result cannot be attributed to the March oil-price break alone. Second, BDC evidence improves as portfolio measurement becomes more granular: the firm-fixed-effects equity coefficient progresses from $-0.062$ for a coarse brown share to 0.152 for estimator-aligned DCC--FF49 portfolio beta. A broader, pre-specified KOL continuation improves factor tracking but not inference, showing that the remaining gap is primarily a disclosure-and-power problem rather than only a factor problem. Third, the maintained climate scenario reduces the BDC statutory asset-coverage buffer by 5.85 percentage points without a primary-scenario breach.

\section{Data and Method}

\subsection{Samples and portfolio exposure}

CRSP supplies daily returns and market equity; Compustat supplies quarterly balance-sheet variables; the CCM table supplies identifiers. The 2010--2025 market archive contains the ten benchmark banks and twenty BDCs. Bank of New York Mellon is recovered manually with PERMNO 49656 and SEC CIK 0001390777 because the restricted code extracts do not provide a usable link. The BDC exposure panel contains 19 firms and 380 firm-quarters from 2021Q1 through 2025Q4. BBDC remains in the return archive but is excluded from exposure regressions because its filing parser does not recover a reconciled industry distribution.

BDC industries are parsed from SEC 10-Q and 10-K Schedules of Investments. The archive retains each source row, matching rule, and confidence flag. A fixed dictionary defines narrow energy/materials exposures and a broader measure that adds carbon-intensive transportation, capital goods, machinery, construction, and manufacturing. The broad share averages 10.19 percent and ranges from zero to 38.70 percent.

The main portfolio measure is more granular. I form daily value-weighted returns for Fama--French 49 industries from all eligible NYSE, AMEX, and Nasdaq U.S. common stocks in CRSP \citep{FamaFrench1997}, estimate each industry's climate beta, and weight those betas by the industries disclosed in each BDC portfolio. The public SIC rules classify more than 99.8 percent of eligible stock observations; the BDC label crosswalk maps 95.2 percent of reported portfolio weight overall and 97.5 percent in the median BDC-quarter. Four parser-selected geography tables are replaced with the nearest valid industry quarter and separately excluded in robustness. By comparison, \citet{JungEngleBerner2025} observe about five million Y-14 loans for 19 banks and construct loan-size-weighted borrower-industry climate beta from three-digit NAICS. The BDC application has the same number of institutions but only 380 aggregated institution-quarters, so its information content is materially lower.

\subsection{Climate factor and dynamic beta}

Following \citet{JungEngleBerner2025}, the stranded-asset factor is

\begin{equation}
CF_t=0.70R_{Coal,t}+0.30R_{XLE,t}-R_{SPY,t}.
\label{eq:factor}
\end{equation}

KOL supplies the coal return through 14 December 2020. The strict replication then follows the published rule: a fixed, dollar-converted return on its five largest pre-liquidation holdings. Its daily, weekly, and monthly correlations with KOL are 0.454, 0.832, and 0.931 over the common 2019--2020 window. The article does not specify a periodic refresh rule. I therefore pre-specify a breadth robustness using VanEck's official 30 September 2020 N-PORT schedule: securities are ranked by reported market value until cumulative coverage first exceeds 75 percent. The resulting 15-security basket covers 77.1 percent and raises tracking to 0.790 daily, 0.945 weekly, and 0.973 monthly \citep{VanEckNPORT2020}. The remaining frequency gap is consistent with non-synchronous international closes. Economic drift remains relevant because Adaro separates a thermal-coal unit representing 89 percent of group revenue in December 2024 \citep{IEEFA2025}.

Institution returns are modeled with the market and climate factor using variance-targeted GJR-GARCH(1,1) and scalar DCC(1,1). Let $F_t=(M_t,CF_t)'$. Dynamic coefficients are

\begin{equation}
\begin{bmatrix}\beta^M_{i,t}\\ \beta^C_{i,t}\end{bmatrix}
=\Sigma_{FF,t}^{-1}\Sigma_{Fi,t}.
\label{eq:dcb}
\end{equation}

Equivalently, the climate coefficient is

\begin{equation}
\beta^C_{i,t}=\frac{\sigma_{i,t}}{\sigma_{c,t}}
\frac{\rho_{ic,t}-\rho_{im,t}\rho_{mc,t}}{1-\rho_{mc,t}^{2}}.
\label{eq:climatebeta}
\end{equation}

All 30 institution systems converge; median DCC persistence is 0.9966, implying a 203-trading-day half-life. All 49 industry GJR systems also converge. The main FF49 measure uses the same median scalar-DCC parameter vector for industry and BDC betas; a 252-day industry rolling OLS is retained as a measurement sensitivity. Weekly robustness re-estimates the full model from compounded Friday-week returns rather than averaging daily beta. A three-factor check adds the excess return of HYG/JNK over SHY. Asset beta de-levers equity beta by $E/(E+D)$ and therefore does not use portfolio-industry weights.

\subsection{CRISK, exposure regressions, and BDC capacity}

For a six-month climate-factor decline of $\theta=0.50$,

\begin{equation}
\LRMES_{i,t}=1-\exp\left[\beta^C_{i,t}\log(1-\theta)\right],\qquad \theta=0.50.
\label{eq:lrmes}
\end{equation}

and bank CRISK is

\begin{equation}
\CRISK_{i,t}=kD_{i,t}-(1-k)(1-\LRMES_{i,t})E_{i,t},\qquad k=0.08,
\label{eq:crisk}
\end{equation}

with $k=0.08$ and $m\CRISK=(1-k)E\times\LRMES$. Following the article, aggregate CRISK sums only institution-level shortfalls,

\begin{equation}
\CRISK^{Agg}_t=\sum_i \max(\CRISK_{i,t},0).
\label{eq:aggregatecrisk}
\end{equation}

The replication reports only CRISK and $m$CRISK in the paper; the untruncated institution-level quantity is retained in the code solely as an identity audit.

The main BDC portfolio regression relates standardized traded climate beta to standardized DCC-FF49 portfolio climate beta, includes BDC and quarter fixed effects, and clusters standard errors by BDC. Controls, coarse brown-share models, FF12 and rolling-OLS measures, post-2021 estimation, mapping-confidence filters, and wild-cluster inference are retained as prespecified diagnostics. With only 19 clusters, conventional and small-cluster inference are reported separately.

Reported asset coverage is available for 376 firm-quarters; 375 use a 150-percent threshold and five use 200 percent. The BDC stress mapping is

\begin{equation}
AC^{stress}_{i,q}=AC_{i,q}-100\left(\frac{E_{i,q}\LRMES_{i,q}}{D_{i,q}}\right).
\label{eq:coverage}
\end{equation}

The baseline buffer is $AC-T$. Equation~\eqref{eq:crisk} is retained only for the bank replication; its $k=8\%$ parameter is not treated as a BDC capital requirement. Robustness uses month-end LRMES, book equity, a market-factor placebo, and each factor's empirical first-percentile six-month return ($-34.1$ percent for climate and $-16.8$ percent for the market).

\section{Finding 1: Replication Fidelity}

\tabref{tab:h1} and \figref{fig:annualbeta} show that mean bank climate beta rises from 0.193 in 2019 to 0.424 in 2020. The paired increase is 0.230 (standard error 0.029), and all ten changes are positive. The daily diagnostic gives the same point estimate with HAC(203) standard error 0.104. Both estimates are significant at conventional levels. These statistics establish a common directional movement across institutions; because all banks face the same factor realization, they are not ten independent event replications. The twenty-BDC validation is similarly positive: the mean change is 0.177 (standard error 0.023), with 19 of 20 changes positive. \figref{fig:bankcross} displays the institution-level changes and \figref{fig:benchmarkplot} displays the replication ratios.

The magnitude comparison is also close. End-2020 top-four $m$CRISK is \$221.7 billion versus the published \$260.0 billion; the CRISK increase is \$372.8 billion versus \$430.89 billion. The 85.3-percent $m$CRISK ratio equals a 0.972 equity-scale ratio times a 0.877 climate-loss-rate ratio. Panel C preserves the nonlinear identity by computing $0.92E_t\LRMES_t$ daily and then averaging, rather than evaluating LRMES at mean beta.

The annual result does not isolate a single transition event. The raw cross-bank mean is 0.1974 on 9 March, 0.6329157 on 17 March, and reaches 1.0318 on 10 November after the Pfizer announcement. The 17 March value is a raw ten-bank cross-sectional mean; WFC's numerically similar 0.6328319 is a different object---its 31 December 127-day beta. The code audit verifies the two source slices and their $8.37\times10^{-5}$ difference. \figref{fig:bankcrisk} therefore separates the oil, COVID, and vaccine/value-rotation dates rather than assigning the November maximum to the March shock.

\section{Finding 2: Portfolio Measurement and Factor Maintenance}

\tabref{tab:desc} describes the BDC panel, while \tabref{tab:h2main} and \figref{fig:ff49mechanism} show how measurement resolution changes the result. In the firm- and quarter-fixed-effects equity specification, the coefficient is $-0.062$ for the broad brown share, 0.055 for FF12 portfolio beta, 0.103 for rolling-OLS FF49 beta, and 0.152 for estimator-aligned DCC--FF49 beta. The last estimate has a clustered standard error of 0.085 and is significant at 10 percent under conventional two-sided inference. It remains 0.163 when low-confidence mappings are excluded and 0.196 after dropping 2021. With 19 clusters, however, the equity wild-cluster $p$-value is 0.153.

The original bank validation is much stronger, but it tests a more informative exposure. Its main Y-14 coefficients range from 1.112 to 1.718 and carry three stars in all four specifications. Unlevered-industry, utilized-exposure, and listed-borrower matched versions also carry three stars in every specification; the actual-weight placebo comparison remains significant while shuffled portfolio weights do not. These are not robustness tests of a binary brown-loan share. They preserve continuous climate-beta variation across three-digit NAICS industries or matched borrowers. \tabref{tab:y14compare} reports the published coefficients.

The BDC precision gap has four sources. First, Y-14 contains more than five million loans and 666 bank-quarters; the BDC panel contains 380 public filing summaries. Second, the Y-14 weights are loan-level committed or utilized amounts, whereas BDC labels often pool heterogeneous borrowers. Third, the BDC sample begins after the 2020 repricing and supplies only 20 quarters. Fourth, BDC equity reflects credit spreads, NAV discounts, liquidity, dividends, leverage, and manager effects in addition to portfolio transition risk. A weak coarse-share regression therefore does not show that market climate beta is invalid; it shows that public BDC disclosure cannot reproduce the original loan-level validation design. \tabref{tab:factor} retains the coarse-share classification, timing, power, and high-yield-return diagnostics.

\figref{fig:factorsensitivity} isolates the factor issue. Expanding the continuation from five names to the official cumulative-75-percent basket raises daily KOL tracking from 0.454 to 0.790. The firm-and-quarter fixed-effects equity coefficient rises from 0.152 to 0.184, but its standard error also rises from 0.085 to 0.114; the conventional two-sided $p$-value is 0.124 and the wild-cluster $p$-value is 0.230. Weekly estimation with the same 15 names yields 0.173 (standard error 0.150). Thus the top-five basket was too narrow for a five-year extension, but basket breadth alone does not explain the BDC inference gap. The published top-five rule remains the strict replication, and the 75-percent basket is reported as a pre-specified robustness rather than selected on BDC significance.

\section{Finding 3: BDC Statutory Capacity}

The statutory mapping in Equation~\eqref{eq:coverage} reduces mean coverage from 203.28 to 197.43 percent and the mean buffer from 53.15 to 47.30 percentage points. \tabref{tab:stress} and \figref{fig:coverage} report 5.85 points of mean compression, no primary-scenario breach, and an increase from six to eleven observations within 10 points of the threshold. All reported BDC results use the statutory asset-coverage rule; the bank $k=8\%$ formula is not used to characterize BDC capital adequacy.

The empirical first-percentile climate decline is 34.1 percent and produces 3.63 points of compression. The matched first-percentile market decline is 16.8 percent and produces 10.58 points, so the climate-to-market ratio is 0.343. The 2010--2025 window excludes 2008, making this ratio potentially high relative to a longer market-stress history. A 50-percent market scale placebo produces 33.67 points and 82 breaches. These are calibrated comparisons, not estimated structural losses; stars are uninformative because positive beta mechanically maps into positive compression and first-stage DCC uncertainty is not propagated.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}

The bank replication recovers the annual direction, significance, and 85--87 percent of published CRISK magnitudes. For BDCs, continuous FF49 portfolio beta is more informative than a brown-share proxy, but public filing summaries do not reproduce the precision of loan-level Y-14 data. A broader KOL basket materially improves tracking without resolving that inference gap, separating factor maintenance from exposure identification. The BDC statutory exercise records meaningful but non-breaching buffer erosion. Code, outputs, and audit instructions are available at \url{https://github.com/MaryMai233/crisk-bdc-replication}.
